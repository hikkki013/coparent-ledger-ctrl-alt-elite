## Customer and recurring job

The intended buyer is an individual US co-parent aged 25-45 who regularly shares child expenses and has a co-parent willing to coordinate. This is the Money/B2C positioning hypothesis. Receipts, proposed shares, decisions, partial reimbursements and confirmation of receipt need a traceable connection.

One billing household means one co-parenting pair and its shared children, even when the adults live apart. Count the pair once; parents and children are not separate subscriptions. One adult's payment does not supply the other's consent or change financial permissions.

## Why a family might renew

The return hypothesis is the next school, medical or activity expense, an unfinished reimbursement, or a need to review previous decisions. Per-child statistics and future estimates provide context; plans do not create debt. Saved guest progress is an implemented return mechanism, not measured paid retention.

Measure a second completed expense cycle first. A later, separately authorized commercial launch would measure first payment and month 1/3/6 renewal by acquisition cohort, along with refunds, support time and churn reasons. Demo unlocks and price clicks are not revenue.

## Economics and alternatives

At $7.99/month, one continuously paying household generates $95.88 annualized gross subscription revenue. The [full model](MARKET_AND_ECONOMICS.md) gives conservative/base blended CAC of $52.50/$25, monthly churn assumptions of 20%/12%, and contribution LTV of $25.70/$52.84. These are conditional calculations, not operating metrics. The conservative case and broad paid acquisition fail; fixed costs are excluded from contribution LTV. A $100m ARR target needs 1,042,971 active paid households, beyond the modeled initial SAM.

Existing services already offer expense coordination, and 2houses already charges for a family. Our positioning is an expense-first browser workflow at a clear household price, not a claim of uniqueness or proven superiority. Official competitor links and price units are in the report. Free spreadsheets/messages remain a plausible substitute.
