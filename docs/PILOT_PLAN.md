# Proposed pilot: 5-10 pairs of parents

Status: a research plan only. No participants are recruited, no messages or invitations have been sent, no study results exist, and billing remains disabled. Dates begin only after the implementation is ready and both adults voluntarily choose to participate.

## What the pilot should establish

Test whether two adults will jointly use a narrow expense ledger, complete repeated cycles independently, and value it enough to consider the stated $7.99 monthly household price. Do not test or claim legal compliance, conflict reduction, court acceptance, bank verification, or financial enforcement.

Recruiting unit and analysis unit: one pair/household. Ten individual accounts are not ten customers. Both adults must be willing participants; paying or joining does not authorize action on the other's behalf.

## Before real-family use

First run usability sessions using synthetic expenses and SAMPLE receipts. Real personal information should not be requested while real Auth/RLS/Storage, isolation, persistence, or recovery checks are blocked.

Before a real-data pilot, require documented live two-account and third-household isolation checks, exact balances, concurrent/repeated command behavior, private frozen receipt access, and a reviewed recovery/retention/deletion and support process. Explain the lack of corrections for already approved expenses or confirmed payments. If these conditions are not met, keep the pilot synthetic and do not describe it as live household adoption.

Use aliases instead of child identifiers. Do not collect diagnoses, custody details, SSNs, school addresses, or copies of legal documents. Receipt content may still be sensitive, so participants should avoid unnecessary details. No receipt, free-text concern, session token, or child alias belongs in third-party analytics or AI services.

## Participants and sequence

Seek 5-10 US pairs, primarily ages 25-45, already sharing child expenses and willing to review a tool together. Do not pressure a reluctant second parent. Discovery/outreach is a later separately authorized task; this plan does not send anything.

| Stage | Activity | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Baseline | Ask both adults how they currently track expenses and what happened in the last reimbursement cycle | Current tool, number of recent cycles, self-reported time and specific difficulties |
| Synthetic onboarding | Each adult uses their own session; create a sample expense, concern/approval, and partial transfer confirmation | Observed task completion, errors, assistance needed |
| Invitation | Initiating adult invites the consenting partner; recipient accepts themselves | Acceptance within seven days, with a defined denominator of eligible invitations |
| First cycle | Pair completes a cycle without the team acting as either adult | Submitted proposal, reviewer decision, report, recipient confirmation |
| Repeat | Observe a later distinct expense cycle within 14 days when one naturally occurs | Repeat household usage; separate no-new-expense cases from abandonment |
| Price discussion | Present exactly $7.99/month for both adults and ask what they would choose | Decline, uncertain, or explicit intent; reasons and alternatives |
| Follow-up | If future billing is actually implemented, separately offer an authorized purchase and later observe renewal | Actual paid households and renewals; never infer from a survey |

The proposed 14-day no-card trial is a commercial hypothesis. No countdown or subscription entitlement is currently enforced by billing. Do not say people purchased or renewed during this MVP.

## Metrics and denominators

| Metric | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| Invitation acceptance | Accepted partner invitations / eligible invitations sent to already consenting participants |
| Activation | Joined pairs completing an approved expense, external report, and recipient confirmation / joined pairs |
| Independent completion | Activated pairs requiring no staff action or step-by-step rescue / activated pairs |
| Repeat use | Pairs completing a second distinct expense cycle / activated pairs with another expense opportunity during observation |
| Proposal resolution | Submitted proposals reaching an explicit approve or concern decision / submitted proposals |
| Coordination effort | Each parent's reported/observed time per cycle; distinguish measured timing from recalled baseline |
| Support burden | Assistance time and number/type of issues per pair |
| Price intent | Pairs explicitly choosing the $7.99 proposal / pairs who saw that exact proposal; not payment conversion |
| Paid conversion later | Households completing an actual authorized purchase / eligible households offered a working paid plan |

Report small-sample counts alongside rates and record missing observations. A long delay between reporting and confirmation may reflect real transfer timing; it is not automatically a software failure or parent misconduct. Do not score or rank parents.

## Directional decision rules

These are internal hypotheses, not statistical proof or industry benchmarks. For a completed cohort of ten pairs, a useful next-step signal would be at least six activated pairs, at least four independently repeating when they have another eligible expense, and at least three voluntarily expressing interest in the exact price. With five pairs, report the counts without pretending the sample is conclusive.

- **Continue testing:** both adults can use the workflow independently and several pairs prefer it to their current approach. Validate actual payment later.
- **Change the workflow:** accepted invitations stall because proposals, confirmations, or balances are misunderstood.
- **Reconsider the offer:** a partner will not join, current tools are sufficient, or no pair considers the price worthwhile.
- **Stop real-data use:** cross-household disclosure, incorrect balances, unauthorized state changes, or inability to understand a confirmed payment. Resolve and verify the issue before resuming.

Record product observations and limitations honestly. Do not turn a proposed pilot or a successful synthetic exercise into claimed customer traction.
